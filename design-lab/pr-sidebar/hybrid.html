What the hybrid takes, and from where

  • Sticky action footer (option 2) — the next action never scrolls away. It restates the blocker and offers the one control that matters, changing with state.
  • Properly disabled merge button (option 6) — greyed and disabled when blocked. Options 1, 3 and 5 paint it red (reads destructive); option 4 paints it blue (reads clickable). Only a real, satisfiable action gets a live button.
  • Inline blocked reason (option 4) — “Merge blocked: 1 approval outstanding” sits directly above the control, so cause and effect are adjacent.
  • Row icons on Change & risk (option 5) — the densest block in the sidebar; icons make eight rows scannable without adding width.
  • Count chips + timestamps (option 6) — 1 of 2, 0 new on the header, so a collapsed section still reports.
  • “Why Medium?” explainer (option 2) — risk is the one number nobody trusts without a reason; it gets one inline.
  • Risk as its own labelled sub-block (option 3’s keeper) — but stacked, not a side-by-side card, which cannot fit 300px.

Two deliberate departures

No sidebar tabs. Options 2 and 4 put Summary / Findings / Merge tabs inside the rail. A second tab bar in 300px, on a page that already has five main tabs, is an IA smell — and Findings would duplicate the Files-changed workspace. The collapse pattern the 360° strip already uses does the same job with no new navigation model.

Strip-aware defaults. Change & risk, Enforcement and Delivery are answered by pills in the 360° strip above, so they start collapsed with their summary on the header row. Nothing is removed — one click opens any of them, and the choice persists.
